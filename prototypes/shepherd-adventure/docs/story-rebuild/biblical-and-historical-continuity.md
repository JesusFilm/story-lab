# Biblical continuity and historical plausibility

Standing user direction, 15 September 2026: make story text, clothing and relevant
environment details believable through biblical continuity and historical review.
This applies to future scene and asset work alongside the standing visual style.

## What the shepherds know

Primary text: [Luke 2, World English Bible](https://ebible.org/eng-web/LUK02.htm),
checked 15 September 2026. The prototype's opening uses this translation.

- **Explicit in Luke 2:10–12:** the birth has already happened, in David's city.
  The angel identifies the baby as Savior, Christ the Lord. The recognizable sign
  is a baby wrapped in cloth and lying in a manger (WEB: feeding trough).
- **Not in the recorded announcement:** the names Mary or Joseph, a donkey,
  a particular house, a shelter owner or directions through the village. The text
  does not establish that the shepherds know the parents' names before arriving.
- **Narrator knowledge:** Luke names Mary and Joseph in 2:16 when describing the
  discovery. Those names are appropriate in scripture/narration and production
  notes; that does not authorize a searching shepherd to use them beforehand.
- **Wording:** use “the couple,” “the mother” or “the child” as appropriate to the
  speaker's established information. “Newborn King” is an interpretation rather
  than the wording of Luke's announcement; this reunion uses “the child.”

## Point 09 continuity decision

The owner says the woman was about to give birth **when the couple came earlier**.
That past-tense report can coexist with the shepherds' knowledge that the birth
has now happened. It does not tell the shepherd the baby has been seen in this
stall; the recognition of the sign still belongs to the shelter scene.

The reunion says the owner gave **the couple** shelter. No names are introduced.
The companions' gate arrival, reunion, owner encounter and village detour are
authored connective fiction, not events asserted by Luke. Their invented dialogue
is not labeled or quoted as scripture in the game.

## Historical review for each scene or asset

1. Read the relevant passage. List what is explicit, inferred and invented.
2. Check the speaker's knowledge and chronology against the preceding scenes.
3. Check visible clothing, footwear, tools, lighting, buildings and customs against
   reliable evidence for the place and period. Use museum collections,
   archaeological publications or scholarly sources for uncertain details.
4. Record source links and practical uncertainty in the scene/asset documentation.
   Follow the established style without calling stylized choices historically proven.
5. Flag material conflicts in other scenes for a focused user review. Approval of
   art or gameplay is not evidence of its historical accuracy.

## Known follow-ups; not resolved by the reunion

- The earlier search asks about a couple travelling with a donkey before an
  on-screen source establishes that lead. Luke does not mention a donkey in the
  birth account. Review how the player learns to look for the couple, and revise
  that question/lead across House 3, House 8 and House 9 as a separate continuity
  pass. This iteration preserves their accepted interactions and artwork.
- Existing costumes, lanterns, lamp-making materials and settlement architecture
  have not received a comprehensive historical audit. This guide does not certify
  them. This reunion reuses the existing character and village assets.
- Recheck the restored opening-to-search handoff before release: the shepherds
  should be searching for an already-born child and the announced sign.
