# Independent quality review of round 07

Historical review: raw iteration captures and recordings were deleted during repository cleanup. The observations below record checks performed at the time; removed evidence is not available for reinspection. Current verification is in [review/latest](../review/latest/).

Reviewed the round 06 baseline in `review/07-carpentry/before/` and the saved desktop and phone keyframes for candidates 01, 02, 03, and 04. I did not implement this pass and did not launch a browser. The keyframes establish staged poses only; continuous motion, response latency, and audio remain unassessed.

**Retain candidate 04.** Its phase 1 mallet head visibly reaches the top of the loose plank at both 1366px and 360px. The raised phases retain a readable tool silhouette, while the intermediate poses give the action a clear wind-up, strike, and recovery sequence. Moving the bench behind and beneath Noah also makes the character read as working at it instead of standing beside a detached prop. This directly repairs round 06's strongest defect.

Reject the earlier candidates. Candidate 01 loses the tool and produces an incoherent folding mesh. Candidate 02 keeps the new arm construction but leaves the mallet floating above the work. Candidate 03 improves alignment, yet the mallet head is largely hidden by the bench edge at contact. Candidate 04 gives the clearest contact without introducing a new obstruction in the phone composition.

The rigid partition between the unchanged painted body and animated forearm remains coherent at normal capture scale. The sleeve, cuff, hand, and mallet keep plausible attachment through phases 0.65, 1, and 1.6. Enlarged inspection reveals a slightly stiff elbow/cuff transition, especially across the chest in the intermediate frames, but no obvious flap, duplicate limb, or continuous-mesh collapse appears in the saved stills.

**Strongest remaining defect:** the strike is readable but mechanically light. The mallet touches the loose plank without visible compression, rebound, wood response, or a strongly planted working posture. That is acceptable for restrained storybook motion, though it falls short of expressive character acting. Further work should preserve the clean contact and improve the pose or tiny wood response; it should not add broad sprite sway or distort the authored body.

Fixed five-point rubric: 1 absent/broken, 2 rudimentary, 3 coherent but limited, 4 polished with minor shortcomings, 5 directly demonstrated reference-level. Scores are review judgments, not user approval.
