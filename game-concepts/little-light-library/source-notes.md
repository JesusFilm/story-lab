# StoryComet reference notes

## Evidence and limits

The video was replayed and reviewed through visual snapshots across playback.
Computer use supplied images and accessibility text, not an audio transcript;
these notes do not claim to transcribe or verify the spoken commentary.

Visible presentation included a wooden bookshelf, dimensional books opening onto
a table, illustrated pop-up scenes, a separate reading panel, page controls,
reading-mode buttons, progress ribbons, a souvenir collection and decorative
objects on the shelf. Later scenes showed Japanese story text, highlighted text,
a quiz entry button and a five-language menu. Sparkling pointer effects and page
transitions made the interface feel toy-like and tactile.

The creator's post describes 15 animated books, translated narration and interface,
parent progress tracking, quizzes, pins, 3D souvenirs and language switching.
Those are creator claims, not independently tested features. The post describes
rapid AI-assisted production; it does not establish learning effectiveness.

## What Story Lab takes forward

The promising pattern is a story becoming a place to visit and an object to remember.
The [concept](concept.md) adds Bible passages, shared family reflection, retelling,
meaningful recall cues and an authored first experiment. These are proposals,
not observations of StoryComet. No reference artwork or product code was copied.
