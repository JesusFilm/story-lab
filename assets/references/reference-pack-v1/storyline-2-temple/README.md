# Storyline 2 — Where is Jesus?

## Proposed experience: listen, notice, reconnect

Use a compact district with two converging routes and three recognizable places. The player follows Mary with Joseph accompanying her, or switches their viewpoint at rest points. Travel advances automatically along broad paths; a remote selects direction or interaction. Walking stops at conversations. No timed pursuit, life counter or combat is needed.

| Beat | Visual clue and interaction | Progress and default |
|---|---|---|
| Travelers' rest | Listen as the parents check among relatives and companions. | Establish return to Jerusalem through a transition; default continues the story. |
| Entrance courtyard | A well, ochre market canopy and olive tree provide distinct visual anchors. | Choose market or olive court; both can reach the Temple steps. |
| Market witness | A person becomes visibly attentive when approached. Select “ask” or listen; their gesture points toward the broad stairs. | Record a voiced and pictorial clue; default asks and continues after the conversation. |
| Olive court | Another traveler recalls a boy interested in conversations about Scripture. Nearby voices and architecture suggest the colonnade. | Optional corroborating clue, available before or after the market; no mandatory item hunt. |
| Temple steps | A visitor indicates a group listening nearby. A conversational sound draws attention under the portico. | Both routes converge. Provide captions and visible gestures so sound is never the only cue. |
| Portico and reunion | Jesus sits among teachers, listening and asking questions. Parents enter with relief. | Stop navigation, allow an unhurried story scene and a short reflection. |

Witnesses, recollections, clue order and dialogue are invented connectors for this prototype. They are not quotations from the Gospel. Do not require the boy to have lost personal belongings; the account describes his intentional presence in the Temple.

### Search without frustration

- Inspect/ask choices remain available while the character is stopped. Missed remote input cannot cause a clue to expire.
- Clues are replayable, combine speech and pictures, and are not distinguished solely by color. Avoid small text or tiny hidden objects.
- Paths allow return and converge naturally. Visiting one witness can be sufficient; extra witnesses add texture. No fixed order or duplicate fetch quests.
- If the viewer makes no choice, a companion suggests the next place and the route continues. A held pause can wait indefinitely. An optional “help me find the next clue” highlights a person or landmark.
- Proposed duration is 5–8 minutes, including three short conversations and the reunion. This is a design target; this storyline has no verified navigation graph or measured timings yet.

## Layout and image-to-3D handoff

The district image proposes a compact game microcosm, not a map of historical Jerusalem. Start with a roughly 70 × 90 m blockout. Use the two courtyards as distinct spaces linked to the same stair landing. Test walking width, camera clearance and sightlines before adding detail.

Suggested reusable assets: straight/corner limestone walls, flat-roof attached houses, timber doorframes, well, ochre cloth canopy and posts, amphorae and storage jars, olive tree, low benches, broad stairs, column-and-lintel portico, scroll prop. Keep scene lighting and camera references separate from any subsequent isolated asset reconstruction. These images are not orthographic views, calibrated meshes or texture maps.

Maintain character continuity with the generated travelers' rest scene: Mary in dusty blue and cream; Joseph in clay brown and sand. Jesus should visibly read as a boy around twelve, with no beard and child proportions. The Temple setting is a public court/portico rather than the inner sanctuary. Exact building geometry, costumes, calendar-phase architecture and character ages beyond Jesus's stated age remain artistic interpretations.

## Narrative basis

Luke 2:41–52 places the visit at Passover, when Jesus was twelve. His parents initially assume he is with the traveling company; they search among relatives and acquaintances and return to Jerusalem. They find him after three days, among teachers, listening and asking questions. A short game compresses that narrated time through scene transitions; it should not imply the whole event took a few minutes.
